What the rankings establish

What these maps measure

The heat layer uses all 12,000 supplied coordinate/weight samples for the selected zone. The export does not document the weight formula. Colour indicates smoothed weighted sample concentration, not a calibrated congestion rate or an investment score.

The replay follows the original lane shapes and junction connections between one-second observations. Recorded positions, IDs, stops and speeds are retained. A vehicle keeps the same body colour; colour does not indicate speed. The 3D car model is illustrative. Between-sample lane changes and steering remain a reconstruction, not a new SUMO run.

The original network, route files and FCD recordings were recovered from the study files. This view replays the archived simulation; it does not rerun the traffic model. Billboard visibility geometry and audience measurements are not provided, so it cannot calculate advertising exposure.

CPM = price per 1,000 impressions. Example: 10,000 impressions × MAD 55 / 1,000 = MAD 550.

Counting method and limitations

The white line is a counting gate on one lane. A crossing is confirmed when the same vehicle passes it between consecutive observations. Time is interpolated within one second; lane changes and missing samples are excluded. Counts may therefore understate crossings and do not cover the entire road.

A vehicle can pass more than once. Impressions are not unique people. Pausing, rewinding or changing playback speed adds no extra crossings.

FROM TRAFFIC TO A DECISION

Why simulate cars to choose a billboard?

Traffic helps compare site conditions: vehicle movements, direction and slowdowns. The investment decision then weighs estimated advertising audience against selling prices and costs.

  1. 01

    Observe traffic

    SUMO provides vehicles, trajectories and speeds. The selected road segment makes these conditions readable locally.

  2. 02

    Estimate audience

    Panel orientation, obstructions, visibility, occupants and display duration still need checking. One car is not one advertising impression.

  3. 03

    Sell advertising

    CPM is the price per 1,000 advertising impressions. At a CPM of MAD 55, 10,000 billable impressions represent MAD 550 in revenue.

  4. 04

    Test profitability

    High revenue is not enough: rent, energy, maintenance and the initial investment must also be covered.

Two formats, two ways to sell

A · Static OOH

The poster stays up throughout the campaign. The thesis scenario uses MAD 22,500 per month and 90% occupancy: MAD 243,000 annual revenue. After MAD 258,743 in annual costs, the balance is −MAD 15,743.

B · Digital DOOH

The screen shares its loop between advertisers. The thesis assumes six ten-second slots. Selling them requires an audience attributable to each ad: six slots do not automatically deliver six times the viewers.

Sources and calculation choices

Table 27 (p. 93) provides costs; pp. 94–95 provide revenue assumptions. The thesis obtains MAD 831,600/year from 300,000 monthly impressions × 6 slots, a CPM of MAD 55 and 70% fill. Its audience basis is not explicit: per-slot mode reproduces that result, while screen mode allocates the total only once. Alternative cost bases and ROI inconsistencies later in the thesis are not carried into this calculation.

The 4 × 3 m billboards and locations illustrate the method. They are not surveyed panels or authorised or recommended sites. Road segments do not model actual visibility. The financial scenario stays the same across zones unless its assumptions are edited.

OOH vs DOOH Investment Appraisal

For my final-year project at 2ACOM, I compared static billboards and digital screens over five years using their costs and assumed revenue.

Formats compared
4 × 3 m · static & LED
Locations
Premium & secondary
Appraisal horizon
Five years

The thesis connects SUMO traffic simulation across five Casablanca zones with a comparison of static and digital advertising. The financial analysis examines installation, rent, energy, maintenance and advertising occupancy.

Method, assumptions & interpretation
  • Traffic

    One-hour simulations with virtual E1 detectors. Traffic counts support location analysis but do not measure advertising impressions.

    Simulation method, pp. 36–38
  • Financial appraisal

    Compare total ownership cost, occupancy assumptions, break-even and discounted cash flows. The paper uses several cost baselines, which must be reconciled before reusing its return estimates.

    Financial scenarios, pp. 67–99
  • Location priorities

    The proposed location score gives 40% to audience potential, 30% to cost efficiency and 30% to revenue potential. The ten-location ranking is explicitly hypothetical.

    Location scoring, pp. 100–103

Interpretation: The digital format has higher revenue potential only if several advertising slots can be sold while covering its higher costs. A real-site appraisal still requires measured audience data and verified commercial assumptions.
